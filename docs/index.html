Climate controller wiring

Where each wire goes for the cabinet, room and box temperature sensors, the infrared receiver and LED, and the fan relay, how to lay the parts out on a prototype board, and how to run the cables. Pin numbers in include/config.h are GPIO numbers; the board prints them as D27, D32 and so on.

Components facing you, antenna at the top. Clones sometimes move pins, so check the printed labels.

Parts

Prototype board layout

Everything on 3.3 V sits above the ESP32, next to its 3V3 pin; everything on 5 V sits below. The infrared LED driver (Q1) sits under D32 and the relay driver (Q2) under D27, each in a straight line from its pin; the infrared receiver U1 sits at the right edge on 3.3 V, facing out, and J4 carries only the LED; the box sensor header J6 sits in the top-left corner, above the antenna keep-out; the relay header is at the USB end near the power input, and the supply capacitors sit between the GND and 5 V wires. Below the ESP32 every wire runs on top and nothing crosses: the LED+ wire (row 24) and the ground wire (row 26) run left under the bodies of R7 and R2, and R4 and R9 lie across the GND wire the same way. Nothing runs along the board edge, and the holes between J4, J5 and the edge stay free for the plugs. Every part on its holes, for a 7 × 9 cm perfboard (24 × 36 holes) seen from the component side. Power goes by plain wire, no rails: solid lines run on top, dashed lines underneath, seen through the board. The DevKit plugs in by its own pin headers into two female headers, so it can be added and removed. The layout was checked by a script: every connection is present, no two wires on the same side cross, and no wire passes over another connection's solder joint.

Parts on the board

  • J1Cabinet sensor: 3V3 · DATA (TX2) · GND. R1 10 kΩ pull-up, standing.
  • J2Room sensor: 3V3 · DATA (RX2) · GND. R5 10 kΩ pull-up, standing.
  • J6Box sensor: 3V3 · DATA (D23) · GND, in the top-left corner above the antenna keep-out. R10 10 kΩ pull-up, standing.
  • U1Infrared receiver at the right edge, dome facing out: OUT (D4) · GND · VCC on 3.3 V. R6 100 Ω from 3V3 to VCC, C4 10 µF across VCC and GND.
  • J5Relay control: IN · GND · 5V, at the USB end. Marked red: 5 V. Check the order against your relay module's pins before plugging in.
  • Q2Relay driver, under D27: R7 1 kΩ from D27 to the base, R8 10 kΩ standing between base and emitter (keeps the relay off while the ESP32 starts), Q2 pulls the module's IN to GND, R9 10 kΩ lying from the collector across the GND wire to 5 V gives a clean 5 V for off.
  • J4Infrared LED, 2 pins: LED+ · LED−. Marked red: 5 V. R2 1 kΩ from D32 to the base, R3 10 kΩ standing between base and emitter, R4 33 Ω from 5 V to LED+, lying across the GND wire, Q1 switches LED− to GND.
  • C1470 µF across GND (column 19) and 5 V (column 20) beside VIN. Mind the polarity: the stripe (−) goes to column 19.
  • +CRoom for more capacitors in the dashed box: column 19 is GND and column 20 is 5 V, rows 27 to 29. Solder each capacitor across the two holes of one row, − on column 19, + on column 20.

Building it

Transistor pins. Q1 and Q2 go in as E-B-C from the left. A 2N2222 has that order seen from its flat side: flat side facing you, as drawn. A BC547 is C-B-E seen from its flat side: put it in with the flat side away from you. Either way, match the legs to the holes marked E, B and C.

Mounting the DevKit. If its pin headers came loose in the kit, solder them to the DevKit first, pointing down from the component side. Solder two 15-pin female headers on rows 12 and 22 as drawn; the DevKit then plugs in and out. Count the holes between your DevKit's two pin rows first: most 30-pin boards are 10 holes (25.4 mm) apart, as drawn, but clones vary.

Standing resistors. R1, R3, R5, R8 and R10 stand upright on two neighbouring holes: one leg straight down, the other bent back over the top of the body.

Wires under resistors. R2, R4, R7 and R9 lie flat across wires (LED+ and ground under R2 and R7, ground under R4 and R9). Use insulated wire for those runs, or mount the resistors a few millimetres off the board, so their bare leads never touch the wires.

Receiver legs. U1 is drawn for a VS1838B (OUT, GND, VCC from top to bottom with the dome facing right). Other receivers order their legs differently: check its datasheet and match the labels.

Cabling

nearKeep together

  • Pull-up resistors next to their header on the board.
  • A 100 nF capacitor across + and − at each bare sensor (modules have one).
  • The LED transistor and its resistors next to D32. The cable then carries only switched LED current.
  • The 470 µF capacitor, and any you add, right beside VIN and GND.
  • Each cable's ground wire in the same cable as its signal. In a network cable, pair each signal with a ground wire.
  • The receiver's 10 µF (C4) and 100 Ω (R6) right beside it.
  • All 3.3 V parts on one side of the ESP32, all 5 V parts on the other.

apartKeep apart

  • Mains never on the perfboard. It isn't rated for it. The relay module and the fan wiring go in their own compartment, with strain relief and a fused feed.
  • Mains cables at least 10 cm from sensor and receiver cables. Where they must cross, cross at a right angle.
  • Nothing metal near the antenna end: no wires or screws. A metal cabinet blocks Wi-Fi, so mount the board near a plastic panel or outside the cabinet.
  • The room sensor away from the cabinet's exhaust, the fan's airflow and the board itself. The cabinet sensor in the air you want to control, not on top of a hot component.
  • The box sensor away from the ESP32 module and the relay, on a short lead: plugged straight into J6 it would read the module's own heat.
  • The infrared receiver out of direct sunlight and lamp light, which carry infrared noise.

plugsConnectors

  • Male pin headers (spare ones from the kit work) with Dupont jumper leads are fine for J1, J2, J4, J5 and J6. J4 plugged in reversed only leaves the LED dark. Screw terminals or JST-XH hold better if the box gets moved.
  • The same pin order on all three 3.3 V connectors (J1, J2, J6): 3V3, signal, GND.
  • Keep the 5 V ones (J4 infrared LED, J5 relay) apart from the 3.3 V ones, as drawn, and mark them in red. Plain pin headers are not keyed, so a sensor lead plugged into 5 V would damage the ESP32 input.
  • Label J1, J2, J4, J5 and J6 on the board next to each header.

Wiring each part

Cabinet temperature sensor

+ / VCC3V3
− / GNDGND

DHT22 or DHT11: the firmware detects which one from its readings, so there is nothing to configure. A three-pin module already has its pull-up resistor. A bare four-pin sensor needs one:

Room temperature sensor (optional)

DataRX2 (GPIO 16)
+ / VCC3V3 (shared)
− / GNDGND (shared)
  • No setting needed: it's shown as soon as it answers, and as off in the watch line while it doesn't. The model is detected automatically. set fan_use_room off keeps it on display but out of the fan rule.
  • Wired like the cabinet sensor. Up to 2 m of cable is fine with the normal pull-up.
  • Pull up to 3.3 V, never 5 V. The ESP32 pins are not 5 V tolerant.
  • Calibrate the pair: put both sensors side by side for 30 minutes, compare Cabinet and Room in status, and set room_offset to the difference.

Box temperature sensor (optional)

DataD23 (GPIO 23)
+ / VCC3V3 (shared)
− / GNDGND (shared)
  • Measures the air inside the controller's own box. Shown in status and the watch line only; the rules never use it.
  • No setting needed: it's shown as soon as it answers. Check the wiring with sensor test box, calibrate with box_offset. A DHT11 is fine here: it reads to about ±2 °C.
  • Put it on a short lead, away from the ESP32 module and the relay, which are the warm parts.
  • D23 leaves D21 and D22 free as an I2C pair.

Infrared receiver

OUTD4
VCC (through 100 Ω R6; 10 µF C4 to GND)3V3
GNDGND

VS1838B, TSOP38238 or any 38 kHz receiver, on the main board at its right edge (U1), dome facing out. It runs on 3.3 V, so its output goes straight to D4. The 100 Ω and 10 µF filter supply noise from Wi-Fi. The leg order differs between parts. On a bare VS1838B it is usually OUT, GND, VCC looking at the dome; check the datasheet or the module's labels. The LED flashing into the receiver is harmless: the firmware ignores its own transmissions.

Infrared LED

Signal (through 1 kΩ to the transistor base)D32
Supply (through 33 Ω to the LED)VIN
Transistor emitter, 10 kΩ pull-downGND

About 100 mA pulses, normal for an infrared LED such as the TSAL6200. The 10 kΩ pull-down keeps the LED off while the board boots. Test with ir send ac_on; a phone camera shows the flash as purple.

Fan relay

IN (through Q2)D27
VCCVIN (5 V)
GNDGND
Fan mainsCOM + NC

Why the transistor: the module switches on when its input is low, through a transistor fed from 5 V. The ESP32's 3.3 V "high" leaves it half on (its LED glows dimly). Q2 pulls IN fully to 0 V for on, and R9 pulls it to a full 5 V for off. D27 high means relay on, so RELAY_ACTIVE_LOW is false in include/config.h.

Test: after fan off the module's LED must be completely dark; after fan on the relay clicks. A module that switches on with a high input (NPN transistor such as S8050), or a solid-state relay with a 3–32 V input, needs no Q2: wire it straight to D27 and set RELAY_ACTIVE_LOW false.

Fail-safe: the fan goes through COM and NC, so it runs whenever the relay is unpowered: if the board fails, crashes or loses power, the fan keeps the cabinet cool. The firmware energises the relay to switch the fan off (FAN_ON_RELAY_NC true in include/config.h), so the relay is held on most of the time: allow for its coil current, about 70 mA from 5 V. The fan also runs for a moment each time the ESP32 starts, until the firmware takes over. Use a mains-rated relay (typically 10 A, 250 V) inside an enclosure.

Power and ground

  • One 3V3 pin (right column, bottom): wire both 3V3 runs from it. It powers the three sensors and the infrared receiver.
  • VIN (left column, bottom): about 4.7 V when the board runs from USB, a computer port or a phone charger of at least 1 A. It feeds the infrared LED and the relay.
  • Two GND pins, one per side, next to the power pins. Both feed the GND wires.
  • Use a short, decent USB cable and no unpowered hub; status shows Last restart: brownout if the supply sags.

Pins to leave alone

  • D12, between D14 and D13. It sets the flash voltage when the board starts. Pulled high at boot, the board won't start. A slipped jumper wire here is the classic cause of a board that suddenly stops booting.
  • D2, D5, D15 also decide how the chip boots (D2 drives the on-board LED). Fine to leave unconnected.
  • TX0 and RX0 carry the USB console. Don't connect anything to them.
  • D34, D35, VP, VN are input-only, so they can't drive the LED or the relay.
  • Free for later additions: D18, D19, D21, D22 (top row, near 3V3) and D13, D25, D26, D33 (bottom row). D14 sends a short signal at boot, so use it only for an input.
